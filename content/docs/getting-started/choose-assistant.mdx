results using material that is safe to share.

## A useful starting point

| Choose first | When it is a natural fit |
| --- | --- |
| **ChatGPT** | You want a broad general assistant, images, files, research, or the OpenAI plugin ecosystem. |
| **Claude** | You often work through long documents and value careful drafting or analysis. |
| **Gemini** | Your work already lives in Google services or you want close Android and Google Workspace integration. |

These are starting hypotheses, not permanent rankings. Plans, limits, and
features change, and the best result still depends on your task.

## Run your own ten-minute test

1. Pick a harmless one-page document or a short public article.
2. Give all three assistants exactly the same instruction:

```text
Summarize this for a busy reader in five bullets. Separate facts from
recommendations. Point out anything uncertain and do not invent missing data.
```

3. Score each answer from 1 to 5 for factual accuracy, clarity, useful detail,
   and the amount of correction it needs.
4. Check practical fit: Does it connect to the services you use? Does the free
   or paid plan meet your file and usage needs?
5. Keep one assistant as your default, but use another when it clearly fits a task better.

## Common traps

- Comparing different prompts or different source files.
- Picking the longest answer instead of the most reliable one.
- Paying for three plans before testing the free options.
- Treating a confident answer as a verified answer.

## Finished when

- [ ] You have one scored comparison.
- [ ] You can explain why your default fits your work.
- [ ] You know one situation where another assistant is the better choice.
