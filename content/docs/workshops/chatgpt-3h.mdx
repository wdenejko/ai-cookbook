---
title: ChatGPT for everyday work — a 3-hour workshop
description: A complete three-hour workshop plan for a non-technical audience aged 40–50.
---

## Workshop goal

After three hours, participants can:

- explain in simple terms what an LLM is and why it can be wrong;
- build a prompt from a goal, context, data, audience, format, and quality criteria;
- work through dialogue: questions, draft, critique, and revision;
- use a devil’s advocate, multiple perspectives, and a bias audit;
- create and iteratively improve an image;
- distinguish a new chat, Project, Memory, and Skill.

## Assumptions

- Group: 8–14 people, each working at their own computer.
- Participants use neutral or anonymized data.
- About 30% presentation and 70% demonstrations and exercises.
- No technical knowledge is assumed.

## Before the workshop

Ask participants to sign in to ChatGPT, bring one harmless work task and a short
piece of sample text, and avoid personal, confidential, financial, or medical
data. Open the prompt cards and image brief from the Library in separate tabs.

## Agenda

### 0:00–0:15 — Opening and safety

Explain the goal, show the interface, and agree on the rule: never paste data
that should not leave the organization. Run one intentionally vague prompt and
compare it with a prompt that contains a clear goal and audience.

### 0:15–0:40 — How a language model works

Use the “How language models work” lesson. Emphasize that the model predicts a
useful continuation; it does not automatically verify truth. Demonstrate one
fluent but uncertain answer and ask the group how it could be checked.

### 0:40–1:10 — A six-part prompt brief

Introduce goal, context, source data, audience, output format, and quality
criteria. Participants rewrite a vague request using the
[six-part prompt brief](/library/prompt/prompt-brief-pl), then compare results
in pairs.

### 1:10–1:20 — Break

### 1:20–1:50 — Prompting as dialogue

Show why one perfect prompt is not required. Use a five-step loop: ask,
clarify, draft, critique, revise. Demonstrate
[step-by-step interview mode](/library/prompt/guided-interview-pl) and let
participants improve one real task through two iterations.

### 1:50–2:15 — Critical thinking

Separate facts from interpretation. Use the
[devil’s advocate](/library/prompt/devils-advocate-pl) and
[bias audit](/library/prompt/bias-audit-pl). Ask participants to identify
assumptions, missing perspectives, and claims that need a source.

### 2:15–2:25 — Break

### 2:25–2:50 — Generating images

Build a visual brief from purpose, subject, setting, composition, style,
lighting, format, and exclusions. Generate a first image, then change one thing
at a time. Check hands, text, logos, stereotypes, and consistency before use.

### 2:50–3:00 — Organizing future work

Explain when to use a new chat, Project, Memory, or Skill. Participants choose
one repeated task they could turn into a reusable prompt or Skill and finish
with the [participant checklist](/docs/workshops/participant-checklist).

## Facilitator notes

Keep examples concrete and short. Ask “What evidence supports this?” more often
than “Is this answer good?”. End every exercise by naming what the participant
changed and why it improved the result.
