## Eight parts of a visual brief

1. **Purpose** — slide, post, banner, illustration, or product image?
2. **Main subject** — what should attract attention first?
3. **Setting** — where does the scene take place?
4. **Composition** — what belongs on the left, right, and centre?
5. **Style** — photography, flat illustration, watercolour, or editorial art?
6. **Light and colour** — what mood and palette should it have?
7. **Format** — portrait, landscape, square, or 16:9?
8. **Exclusions** — what must not appear?

## Example

```text
Create a horizontal 16:9 illustration for a business presentation about
human–AI collaboration. Show a diverse team aged 40–50 working together at a
table. Use a bright blue-green palette and leave clean negative space on the
right for a heading. Do not include logos, watermarks, or text in the image.
```

## Improve one thing at a time

Keep the subject and composition stable while changing a single variable:
lighting, palette, camera distance, expression, or empty space. This makes it
clear which instruction caused the improvement and helps maintain consistency
across a series.

## Check before publishing

- Does the image match its intended use and aspect ratio?
- Are hands, faces, reflections, and object relationships plausible?
- Is any generated text correct and legible? Prefer adding important text later
  in a design tool.
- Are brands, private data, or copyrighted characters present?
- Does the image reproduce stereotypes about age, gender, profession, or
  disability?
- For a series, are palette, viewpoint, lighting, and character details stable?

## A practical iteration loop

Start with a complete brief, generate one draft, name the largest mismatch,
request one focused edit, and compare the result with the brief. Repeat only
until the image is fit for purpose; do not replace a precise correction with
“make it better”.
