---
title: Think critically with AI
description: Use opposing arguments, multiple perspectives and a bias audit to test an answer before trusting it.
---

AI is most useful when it helps you examine a decision, not when it simply
agrees with you. A good review makes assumptions and uncertainty visible.

<LessonGuide slug="critical-thinking-with-ai" locale="en" />

<Concept title="Use a constructive devil's advocate">

Ask for the strongest case against an idea, but require more than criticism:

> Give me the five strongest arguments against this plan. For each one, explain
> the risk and a way to reduce it.

Then ask for the strongest case in favour. Comparing both sides helps prevent
the model from following the direction implied by your first question.

</Concept>

<Concept title="Look through several pairs of eyes">

Ask how a customer, employee, manager and finance team might see the same
proposal. Each perspective should include benefits, concerns and missing
questions. This will not remove <Term name="bias">bias</Term>, but it can reveal
which voices were missing.

</Concept>

<Concept title="Separate evidence from interpretation">

Request three sections:

- facts contained in the supplied material;
- interpretations and recommendations;
- missing information.

Also ask: “What evidence could change this conclusion?” This makes a polished
answer easier to challenge.

</Concept>

<GoDeeper title="Reduce bias before the answer is produced">

Define evaluation criteria before showing the options, anonymize names when
comparing people or suppliers, and use the same questions for every option.
These steps reduce opportunities for hidden preferences to affect the result.
They do not make the model neutral, so a person still owns the final decision.

</GoDeeper>
