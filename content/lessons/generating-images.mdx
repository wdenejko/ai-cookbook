---
title: Generate images with intention
description: A visual brief controls the subject, composition, style and format — then small edits turn a draft into a usable image.
---

Image generation works best when you describe the job the image must do, not
only what it should contain.

<Concept title="Write a visual brief">

Name the use, subject, setting, composition, style, colours, lighting, aspect
ratio and exclusions. For a presentation, say where the title will go and ask
for clear negative space. For a series, repeat the same palette, perspective and
level of detail.

</Concept>

<Concept title="Describe placement, not just mood">

“A modern image about AI” leaves almost every decision to the model. A more
useful direction is: “Three colleagues on the left, eye-level camera, natural
office, soft daylight, 40% empty space on the right, no text or logos.”
Concrete placement gives you a layout you can actually use.

</Concept>

<Concept title="Edit one thing at a time">

Choose the strongest draft, then make a focused change:

> Keep the people, camera angle and layout unchanged. Change only the colour
> palette to warmer tones.

For text-heavy graphics, generate the visual with empty space and add the final
copy in a presentation or design tool. Always proofread any text created inside
an image.

</Concept>

<GoDeeper title="Check visual bias">

Models can fall back to familiar stereotypes about age, gender, jobs and
authority. Describe the people and roles intentionally, then ask what
assumptions the image appears to make. Do not use “diverse” as a substitute for
a clear, respectful brief.

</GoDeeper>
