---
title: How language models work
description: A language model predicts what should come next, which explains both its fluency and its mistakes.
---

You do not need to understand the engineering behind AI to use it well. One
simple mental model is enough: an AI assistant is an excellent pattern maker,
not a person and not a database.

<LessonGuide slug="how-llms-work" locale="en" />

<Concept title="What is an LLM?">

An <Term name="llm">LLM</Term>, or large language model, has learned patterns from
a very large collection of text and other data. When you send it a message, it
generates a response one small piece at a time by predicting what should come
next. This lets it write, summarize, compare and explain with impressive
fluency.

</Concept>

<Concept title="Why does context matter?">

Imagine asking a talented temporary colleague to prepare a document. If you only
say “write an email,” they must guess the purpose, audience and tone. The model
has the same problem. Your prompt, the earlier messages and any attached files
form its <Term name="context-window">working context</Term>. Better context means
less guessing.

</Concept>

<Concept title="Why can it be confidently wrong?">

The model is built to produce a plausible continuation, not to guarantee that
every sentence is true. It can create a convincing but false date, quotation or
source. This is called a <Term name="hallucination">hallucination</Term>. Use AI
freely for drafts and ideas, but verify important names, numbers, dates and
claims.

</Concept>

<GoDeeper title="A useful three-part mental model">

Think of the model as:

1. **a generator** — it creates a likely answer;
2. **a transformer** — it rewrites material you provide;
3. **a thinking partner** — it helps examine options.

It is not automatically a source of current facts. When facts matter, give it
reliable material or ask for sources and check them yourself.

</GoDeeper>

## Try it

Ask the same question twice: first with no context, then with the audience,
purpose and desired format. Compare what the model had to guess in each version.
