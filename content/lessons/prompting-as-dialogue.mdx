---
title: Prompting as a conversation
description: Better results come from a short brief, useful questions and deliberate improvement — not one perfect sentence.
---

A good prompt does not have to be long or clever. It needs to tell the model what
success looks like. For harder tasks, let the model interview you before it
starts.

<LessonGuide slug="prompting-as-dialogue" locale="en" />

<Concept title="Build a six-part brief">

Include the pieces that matter:

1. **Goal** — what needs to be achieved?
2. **Context** — what is happening?
3. **Input** — what material should the model use?
4. **Audience** — who will use the result?
5. **Format** — what should the output look like?
6. **Criteria** — how will you judge quality?

You rarely need all six in a simple request. Add the details that prevent a
costly guess.

</Concept>

<Concept title="Ask the model to interview you">

When you are unsure what belongs in the brief, use an interview:

> Ask me up to five questions before you begin. Ask one question at a time and
> wait for my answer. Then summarize the brief and ask me to confirm it.

This turns prompting into a guided conversation and makes missing information
visible before the model produces a long answer.

</Concept>

<Concept title="Improve one thing at a time">

Treat the first answer as a draft. Ask for a specific change: shorten the
introduction, make the tone warmer, add a comparison table, or separate facts
from assumptions. Changing one dimension at a time makes it easier to see what
actually helped.

</Concept>

<GoDeeper title="A reliable five-step loop">

**Brief → questions → draft → critique → revision**

Finish by asking the model to check the revision against your criteria. This is
more reliable than repeatedly saying “make it better,” because “better” has no
shared definition.

</GoDeeper>
