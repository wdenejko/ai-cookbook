---
title: Recognize deepfakes and AI-assisted scams
description: Pause under pressure, verify through a separate channel and protect yourself from cloned voices, altered media and convincing messages.
---

AI can make a fraudulent message more natural, copy a familiar voice or alter a
photo and video. You do not need to become a deepfake expert. You need a
procedure that remains safe even when the content looks and sounds convincing.

<LessonGuide slug="recognizing-ai-scams" locale="en" />

<Concept title="Recognize the pressure pattern">

Be cautious when a message combines urgency, fear or secrecy with a request for
money, access, a verification code or an unusual action. A familiar voice,
profile picture, writing style or caller ID is no longer enough to establish
identity.

The story may be sophisticated, but the pressure pattern is often simple:
**act now, do not check, use this payment method**.

</Concept>

<Concept title="Verify through a separate channel">

Stop the conversation. Contact the person or organization using a number,
address or app you already know—not the link or number supplied in the message.
Ask a question whose answer is not publicly available. Families can agree on a
private verification phrase for emergencies.

The US Federal Trade Commission gives the same core advice for
[AI voice-cloning scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/scammers-use-ai-enhance-their-family-emergency-schemes):
do not trust the voice; call the person on a known number.

</Concept>

<Concept title="Check origin before appearance">

Look for the earliest available source, not only visual glitches. Search for the
same image or quotation, inspect the account and date, and compare the claim
with an official channel. Cropped, slowed or relabelled authentic material can
mislead without being generated by AI.

Detection tools may help, but none should be the only test.

</Concept>

<Concept title="Protect the next step">

Never share a one-time code, password or remote access because an unexpected
caller asks. Do not pay with gift cards, cryptocurrency or a new bank account
under time pressure. Preserve the message, report it through the platform or
organization and warn the person being impersonated.

If money or account access has already been sent, contact the bank or service
immediately through its official channel.

</Concept>

<GoDeeper title="Use the PAUSE procedure">

Remember five actions:

1. **Pause** the urgent interaction.
2. **Assess** what is being requested.
3. **Use** a known, separate contact channel.
4. **Source-check** the media or claim.
5. **Escalate** to the bank, platform, workplace or authorities when needed.

The procedure works for ordinary impersonation as well as AI-generated media.

</GoDeeper>
