---
title: What is a prompt?
description: A prompt is the text you give an AI, and small changes in wording can change what you get back.
---

Every conversation with an AI starts with a prompt. This short lesson covers what a prompt actually is, why the exact words you choose matter so much, and the difference between the hidden setup instructions and the request you type.

<Concept title="What is a prompt?">

A <Term name="prompt">prompt</Term> is the question or instruction you type so the AI knows what you want. "Write a friendly thank-you note to my landlord about the fixed heater" is a prompt. The more you say about what you need, the closer the answer lands.

</Concept>

<Concept title="Why do the exact words matter?">

An AI cannot read your mind; it only has the words you give it. Compare "explain taxes" with "explain how income tax works to a 15-year-old, in about five short sentences." The second names the audience, the length, and the depth, so the answer is far more useful. Showing an example of a good answer helps even more.

</Concept>

<Concept title="System prompt or your message?">

Most AI apps have two layers. A <Term name="system-prompt">system prompt</Term> is the behind-the-scenes setup ("You are a patient teacher who avoids jargon") that stays fixed for the whole chat. Your message is the specific thing you ask each time. Usually the app's author writes the system prompt and you write the messages.

</Concept>

<GoDeeper title="What actually happens when you press enter?">

Your prompt is split into tokens (small chunks of characters), and the model predicts the next token over and over, feeding each one back in until it decides to stop. Nothing is "looked up" the way a search engine works; the reply is generated fresh. That is why the same prompt can give slightly different answers, and why simply rephrasing can rescue a poor result.

</GoDeeper>
